# Product

## Register

product

## Users

HearO is designed first for Israeli combat veterans, including people who are
currently serving and people who have been discharged, who live with
combat-related sound sensitivity. They know their own triggers, value privacy,
and want a practical tool rather than therapy marketing. The primary workflow
is choosing a familiar scene and consented sounds, completing a short guided
practice session, and retaining control over intensity throughout.

## Product Purpose

HearO provides gradual, consent-based sound practice through ambient scenes,
user-chosen sounds, recorded guidance, and optional pulse-aware softening. It
helps widen the window in which a difficult sound can be experienced as part of
the present environment. Success means the user remains informed, capable, and
in control while the app responds quietly to signs of distress. HearO is a
wellness tool with a therapeutic framework, not a diagnostic product, emergency
service, or replacement for clinical care.

## Brand Personality

Quiet, grounded, capable. The interface should feel steady and lived-in, with
plain language and no drama. It respects the user's judgment, avoids
patronizing reassurance, and keeps privacy and control visible.

## Anti-references

- Military imagery or achievement language, including camouflage, ranks,
  medals, flags, dog tags, silhouettes, and hero framing.
- Generic wellness-app tropes that treat the user as fragile or promise a cure.
- Alarming medical, crisis, biometric, or diagnostic language.
- Gamification, streak pressure, social comparison, and celebratory badges.
- Loud, high-contrast, or notification-heavy interfaces that compete with the
  session or increase activation.

## Design Principles

1. Keep the user in control. User-set intensity is a ceiling, and automatic
   responses may only make the experience softer.
2. Preserve calm under load. Important state changes should be legible without
   popups, alarms, or visual drama.
3. Treat consent as an ongoing interaction. Sounds, permissions, and personal
   data must be chosen deliberately and remain easy to revisit.
4. Use capable, direct language. Avoid therapy jargon, military framing, and
   copy that defines the person by a condition.
5. Keep private data local unless the user explicitly chooses to share it.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

The app is Hebrew-first and fully bilingual in Hebrew and English, including
RTL layout, culturally appropriate copy, and the English-only HearO wordmark.
Controls need clear screen-reader labels and values, large touch targets,
non-color state cues, predictable focus order, and reduced-motion behavior.
Critical support access remains one tap away without visually alarming the
user. Text and controls should meet WCAG AA contrast expectations, and audio
controls must explain their effect without relying on hearing a preview alone.
